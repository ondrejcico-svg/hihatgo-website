Connect an electronic drum kit to an Android tablet

Most electronic drum modules send MIDI over USB: every time you hit a pad, the module tells the tablet which drum it was and how hard you hit it. Android understands this without any drivers. All you need is the right cable.

What you need

  • An electronic drum kit or pad controller with a USB port that sends MIDI. Most modules from Alesis, Roland, Yamaha, Millenium and others do. Check the manual for "USB MIDI".
  • An Android tablet or phone with USB-C (or micro-USB) that supports USB OTG. Almost all modern ones do. A tablet is easier to read from behind the kit.
  • A cable: most modules have a square USB-B port. Use a USB-B to USB-C cable, or the module's own USB cable plus a small USB-C OTG adapter.

Connecting, step by step

  1. Switch the drum module on.
  2. Plug the cable into the module's USB port and into the tablet.
  3. Open HiHatGo!. If Android asks for permission to use the USB device, tap OK.
  4. Go to Settings → Drum kit and hit each pad. The matching drum lights up on the screen.
  5. If a pad lights up the wrong drum, tap the right drum in the picture, tap Assign a pad and hit the pad.
  6. Run the delay calibration once in Settings → Audio & latency.

Tested with: the Alesis Nitro Pro over USB-OTG on a Samsung tablet. If your kit doesn't work, please email hihatgo@gmail.com with the module's name and we'll look at it.

Hearing the song and your drums together

You want to hear the song and your drum sounds at the same time, with as little delay as possible. Options:

  • Through the module (best): some modules take sound over USB. In Settings → Audio & latency → Song plays on, pick Drum module (USB cable), and you hear everything in the module's headphones. The line under the button says if your module supports it.
  • Tablet speakers or wired headphones, with the module's sound on its own headphones or speaker.
  • A cable from the tablet into the module's AUX / line-in, if your module has one.
  • Bluetooth headphones are not recommended: they add a noticeable delay to the music.

Hits feel late? Fixing latency

Every kit, cable and tablet adds a few milliseconds of delay. HiHatGo! judges hits within a few hundredths of a second, so it's worth getting right:

  1. Calibrate once in Settings → Audio & latency.
  2. Avoid Bluetooth audio while playing.
  3. If only one song feels off, the chart itself may be out of time: Song settings → Timing → nudge or Auto-detect.
  4. Switch on No notifications while you play so pop-ups don't interrupt.

The kit isn't found, or stops working

  • Check the module is switched on before you plug in, then unplug and plug in again.
  • Try another cable. Some cheap USB cables only charge and can't carry data.
  • Make sure you're using an OTG-capable adapter if you use one.
  • Some tablets get hot during long sessions and throttle USB. A cooler room or a tablet stand that lets air around it helps.
  • Still stuck? Email hihatgo@gmail.com with your tablet and module model.

Is there a version for PC or Mac?

Not yet. HiHatGo! is for Android tablets and phones. If you mainly practise at a computer, a cheap Android tablet on a music stand next to your kit works very well.
